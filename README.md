## What Is Spousal Sponsorship in Canada?

Spousal sponsorship is a Canadian family immigration program that allows eligible Canadian citizens and permanent residents to sponsor a spouse or qualifying partner for permanent residence.

The purpose of the program is to help eligible families live together in Canada. The sponsor agrees to provide financial support for the sponsored person according to the applicable undertaking, while the sponsored applicant must meet the relevant immigration requirements.

Canadian immigration rules recognize several types of relationships for sponsorship. These include legally married spouses, common-law partners, and qualifying conjugal partners. Each category has its own eligibility requirements and supporting evidence.

A couple's eligibility depends on more than their relationship status. The sponsor must meet the sponsorship conditions, the relationship must qualify under Canadian immigration rules, and the sponsored applicant must satisfy applicable admissibility requirements.

The official Government of Canada [spousal and partner sponsorship program](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/family-sponsorship/spouse-partner-childre) explains the available pathways and application process.

## 1. Helping Couples Understand Whether They Qualify

One of the first ways a spousal sponsorship consultant may help is by reviewing the couple's circumstances against the program requirements.

Under the general federal rules, a sponsor must usually be at least 18 years old, be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, or be a person registered in Canada under the Canadian Indian Act, and meet the applicable residence and sponsorship conditions.

A Canadian citizen living outside Canada may be able to sponsor a spouse or partner if they demonstrate an intention to live in Canada when the sponsored person becomes a permanent resident. A permanent resident living outside Canada generally cannot sponsor a spouse or partner from abroad.

The sponsor must also satisfy other eligibility conditions. Certain previous sponsorship undertakings, criminal convictions, financial obligations, or receipt of social assistance for reasons other than disability can affect eligibility.

A consultant can help couples identify these issues before they invest time and money in preparing an application.

For example, a Canadian permanent resident who has moved abroad to work temporarily may need to consider the residence requirement before beginning the sponsorship process. Similarly, someone who previously sponsored a spouse may need to review whether an earlier undertaking affects their eligibility to sponsor a new partner.

Reviewing these circumstances early can help couples understand what steps they need to take before submitting an application.

## 2. Explaining the Difference Between Spouse, Common-Law, and Conjugal Sponsorship

Not every couple qualifies under the same relationship category. Selecting the correct category is essential because the evidence and eligibility requirements differ.

### Spousal sponsorship for married couples

A legally married couple may qualify for spousal sponsorship if the marriage is legally valid under the applicable rules in the country where it took place and is recognized for Canadian immigration purposes.

Couples generally need to provide a marriage certificate and other documents demonstrating their relationship. Depending on their circumstances, additional evidence may be necessary to establish that the marriage is genuine.

A consultant can help applicants determine which documents are relevant and whether their marriage documentation raises any questions that should be addressed before submission.

### Common-law partner sponsorship

A common-law partner is generally someone who is not legally married to the sponsor but has lived with them in a marriage-like relationship for at least 12 consecutive months.

Couples need to demonstrate that they meet the cohabitation requirement. Evidence may include shared residential records, joint financial documents, leases, bills, correspondence, and other records that establish a shared life.

The appropriate evidence depends on the couple's circumstances. A joint bank account, for example, may support an application, but no single document automatically proves that a relationship meets every requirement.

A consultant may help the couple assemble a consistent collection of evidence showing the length and nature of their cohabitation.

### Conjugal partner sponsorship

Conjugal partner sponsorship is a more limited category. It generally applies to a partner living outside Canada who has been in a committed, interdependent relationship with the sponsor for at least one year but cannot marry or live together as a common-law couple because of significant circumstances beyond their control.

These circumstances may involve legal, immigration, social, cultural, or other barriers. Simply living in different countries or preferring not to marry is not, by itself, sufficient to qualify.

Because this category has specific requirements, couples should carefully review the official [rules on who can be sponsored](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/family-sponsorship/spouse-partner-children/who-you-can-sponsor) before proceeding.

A qualified consultant may help determine whether the facts support this category or whether another pathway is more appropriate.

## 3. Helping Couples Choose the Appropriate Application Pathway

Canadian spousal sponsorship applications generally fall into two broad categories: the Family Class, commonly associated with overseas sponsorship, and the Spouse or Common-Law Partner in Canada Class.

The appropriate route depends on where the sponsored partner lives, their immigration status, and the couple's circumstances.

### Sponsoring a partner who lives outside Canada

The Family Class pathway may be suitable when the sponsored spouse or partner lives abroad. This can allow couples to pursue permanent residence while living in different countries during the application process.

A consultant may help the couple understand the applicable forms, document requirements, and evidence needed to establish the genuine nature of their relationship.

If the sponsored partner wants to visit Canada while the application is being processed, they must still meet the requirements for temporary entry. A pending sponsorship application does not automatically guarantee a visitor visa or admission to Canada.

### Sponsoring a partner who lives in Canada

The in-Canada class may be appropriate for eligible spouses or common-law partners who live together in Canada and meet the applicable requirements, including relevant temporary resident status conditions or an applicable exemption.

A consultant can help assess whether the couple appears to meet the criteria and explain the documentation needed to demonstrate their shared residence and relationship.

The couple should also consider the sponsored person's current immigration status and whether any separate application or action is needed to maintain that status.

Choosing the correct pathway at the beginning can help couples prepare the right documents and avoid unnecessary confusion during the application process.

## 4. Organizing Evidence That the Relationship Is Genuine

Demonstrating that a relationship is genuine is one of the most important parts of many spousal sponsorship applications.

Canadian immigration authorities need to be satisfied that the relationship is real and not entered into primarily to obtain immigration status. A marriage certificate or relationship declaration may be important, but it does not necessarily tell the full story of a couple's shared life.

A consultant may help couples identify appropriate evidence and organize it into a clear, consistent presentation.

Depending on the circumstances, evidence may include photographs taken at different stages of the relationship, travel records, communication history, records of visits, shared financial responsibilities, joint leases, insurance documents, and evidence that family members or friends know about the relationship.

For couples who have lived apart for long periods, travel records and communication history may help explain how the relationship developed and continued despite the distance.

For couples who live together, joint household records and evidence of shared responsibilities may be relevant.

The goal is not to submit as many documents as possible. It is to provide credible evidence that reflects the actual relationship.

### Why consistency matters

A relationship evidence package should be consistent with the information provided in the application forms.

For example, if a couple states that they began living together in a particular month, their residential records should not show unexplained contradictions. If the couple has spent extended periods apart, they should explain the circumstances accurately rather than leave the officer to guess.

A consultant may help identify gaps or inconsistencies and advise the couple on how to clarify them honestly.

No consultant should encourage applicants to fabricate photographs, create false financial records, invent relationship milestones, or misrepresent the length of their relationship. False information can have serious immigration consequences.

## 5. Preparing a Complete Document Checklist

Spousal sponsorship applications require several documents from the sponsor and the person seeking permanent residence. The exact checklist depends on the relationship category, country of residence, family circumstances, and other factors.

A consultant can help couples develop a personalized checklist so they understand which documents they need and when they should obtain them.

Common documents may include:

- Passports and identity documents.
- Marriage certificates or evidence of common-law cohabitation, as applicable.
- Sponsorship forms and permanent residence application forms.
- Proof of the sponsor's Canadian citizenship or permanent resident status.
- Evidence supporting the genuine nature of the relationship.
- Birth certificates and relevant family documents.
- Police certificates when required.
- Immigration history and previous visa or permit information.
- Certified translations and supporting documents where applicable.
- Additional forms or country-specific documents identified by the official checklist.

These examples are not a universal list. Applicants should use the current official application package and personalized checklist to confirm the requirements for their case.

A consultant may also help identify documents that need clarification. If a marriage certificate contains an error, for example, the couple may need to obtain a corrected document or provide an appropriate explanation.

Preparing documents in an organized way can reduce avoidable mistakes and make it easier to respond if immigration authorities request further information.

## 6. Helping Couples Complete the Application Forms Accurately

Immigration forms require careful attention because the information supplied can affect how the application is assessed.

Couples may need to provide details about their relationship history, addresses, previous marriages, children, travel history, employment, and immigration background. The precise questions depend on the forms required for their application.

A consultant may help applicants understand the questions, identify missing information, and check that the answers are consistent with the supporting documents.

For instance, if a sponsored applicant has lived in several countries, their address and travel histories need to be reported according to the instructions. If either partner was previously married, the relevant details and evidence of the end of that marriage may be required.

Applicants should carefully review the completed forms before they are submitted. They remain responsible for ensuring that the information provided in their application is accurate and complete.

The current application process generally involves submitting the sponsorship and permanent residence applications together through the Permanent Residence Portal, following IRCC's instructions.

The official [spousal sponsorship application guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/application-forms-guides/guide-5289-sponsor-your-spouse-common-law-partner-conjugal-partner-dependent-child-complete-guide) explains the required forms, supporting evidence, and application steps.

## 7. Explaining Sponsorship Undertakings and Financial Responsibilities

Many couples assume that the sponsor must meet a specific minimum income to sponsor a spouse. In most cases, there is no minimum income requirement for sponsoring a spouse, common-law partner, or conjugal partner under the federal rules.

However, sponsors must generally agree to provide financial support for the sponsored person and meet their undertaking obligations. Exceptions and additional financial requirements can apply in particular circumstances, including certain cases involving dependent children.

The undertaking is an important responsibility. It is not simply a formality attached to the application.

For spousal sponsorship, the federal undertaking generally lasts three years from the date the sponsored spouse or partner becomes a permanent resident. The sponsor may remain financially responsible under the undertaking even if the relationship ends.

A consultant can explain these responsibilities and help the sponsor understand the commitment before signing the relevant documents.

Couples should review the current official requirements rather than assume that the sponsor's income, employment situation, or financial obligations will be assessed in exactly the same way in every case.

## 8. Providing Guidance for French-Speaking Couples and Quebec Residents

Quebec has a distinct immigration process for certain family sponsorship cases, making it especially important for couples living in the province to understand the applicable provincial requirements.

Federal eligibility rules still matter, but a sponsor living in Quebec may also need to meet Quebec's sponsorship conditions and sign an undertaking with the provincial government.

Quebec's process can involve an additional stage after the federal authorities provide instructions. Sponsors should not submit the provincial undertaking application before the appropriate time, because the province's instructions determine when it can be processed.

Provincial rules and intake arrangements may change. Couples should check the latest instructions from both IRCC and Quebec's Ministry of Immigration, Francisation and Integration (MIFI).

For French-speaking couples, clear communication can also be helpful when reviewing forms, understanding government correspondence, and organizing relationship evidence. Applicants should ensure that any translations meet the relevant requirements and that the information in translated documents matches the originals.

A consultant familiar with the relevant process may help couples identify the steps that apply to their residence and circumstances. However, couples should always verify the current provincial requirements before submitting their applications.

## 9. Identifying Potential Complications Before Applying

Some sponsorship cases are straightforward, while others require more detailed preparation.

Potential complications can include previous sponsorships, past immigration refusals, criminal or admissibility concerns, previous marriages, missing civil documents, periods of separation, or inconsistencies in the relationship history.

A consultant may review the circumstances and help the couple understand what additional documentation or professional advice might be needed.

For example, if the sponsored partner has a previous visa refusal, the couple should review the reasons for that refusal and ensure that the relevant information is disclosed as required. If the couple has limited evidence of cohabitation, they may need to consider which other records accurately demonstrate their circumstances.

If an application involves complex legal issues, the couple may need advice from an authorized immigration professional or a lawyer with relevant expertise.

It is important to address potential concerns honestly. Hiding a previous refusal, omitting required family information, or providing false relationship evidence can create much more serious problems than explaining a difficult circumstance from the beginning.

## 10. Explaining Biometrics, Medical Examinations, and Background Checks

After submitting a spousal sponsorship application, applicants may need to complete additional steps before a final decision is made.

These may include providing fingerprints and a photograph for biometrics, completing an immigration medical examination, or obtaining police certificates where required.

The applicable requirements depend on the applicant's circumstances and IRCC's instructions. Couples should not assume that every applicant follows exactly the same schedule.

A consultant may explain what a request means, how to follow the instructions, and why it is important to respond within the specified period.

Applicants should monitor their online accounts and keep copies of official correspondence. If immigration authorities request additional documents, they should respond accurately and within the deadline provided.

A medical examination or biometrics request does not, by itself, guarantee approval. These are steps in the assessment process, and the application must still satisfy the relevant requirements.

## 11. Helping Couples Understand Application Updates and Processing Times

Waiting for a permanent residence decision can be stressful, particularly when partners are living in different countries or have family plans that depend on the outcome.

Processing times vary according to the application category, location, completeness of the file, verification requirements, and other factors. There is no single timeline that applies to every couple.

A consultant may help explain how to track an application, understand official communications, and identify what action is required if the application status changes.

However, a representative cannot guarantee a particular processing time or influence an officer to approve an application.

Couples should use the official [IRCC processing-time tool](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/check-processing-times) for current estimates and check their application account for updates.

If circumstances change during processing, such as a new address, a change in family composition, or another important development, applicants should follow IRCC's instructions for updating their information.

## 12. Explaining Possible Open Work Permit Options

Some sponsored spouses or common-law partners who are living in Canada may be eligible to apply for an open work permit while their permanent residence application is being processed.

Eligibility depends on the applicable policy and the person's circumstances. A pending sponsorship application does not automatically mean that the sponsored partner can work in Canada.

A consultant may help applicants understand whether a separate work permit application could be available and explain what requirements must be met.

Couples should not assume that an open work permit is available to every person being sponsored, particularly when the sponsored partner is outside Canada or does not meet the relevant conditions.

Applicants should verify the latest instructions directly through the Government of Canada's immigration website before making decisions about employment or immigration status.

## How to Choose a Spousal Sponsorship Consultant in Canada

Choosing an immigration professional is an important decision. Couples should look for someone who explains the process clearly, provides transparent service terms, and understands the applicable sponsorship rules.

Before hiring a consultant, consider asking the following questions:

**Is the professional authorized to provide paid immigration advice or representation?** In Canada, immigration consultants must be members in good standing of the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants (CICC). Lawyers must be members in good standing of the relevant Canadian law society, and Quebec notaries must be members of the Chambre des notaires du Québec.

**What services are included in the fee?** Ask whether the service covers eligibility assessment, document review, application preparation, submission assistance, and responses to additional document requests.

**How will the professional communicate with us?** Couples should understand how they will receive updates and who will respond to questions.

**Will we understand the application before submission?** Both partners should have an opportunity to review the information and supporting documents submitted in their names.

**Are the potential risks explained honestly?** A trustworthy professional should discuss relevant complications and avoid promises that approval is guaranteed.

The Canadian government provides guidance on [choosing an authorized immigration representative](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigration-citizenship-representative/choose/authorized).

## Common Mistakes Couples Should Avoid

One common mistake is assuming that a marriage certificate alone proves the relationship is genuine. While the certificate is important, additional evidence may be needed to establish the nature and history of the relationship.

Another mistake is choosing the wrong category. Common-law sponsorship, for example, has a specific cohabitation requirement, while conjugal partner sponsorship is limited to particular circumstances involving partners who live outside Canada.

Couples may also overlook previous marriages, omit required family members, or provide inconsistent details about addresses and relationship milestones.

Inadequate translations, missing forms, and failure to follow the personalized document checklist can also create delays.

Finally, applicants should be cautious of anyone who promises guaranteed approval, suggests fabricating relationship evidence, or claims to have special influence over immigration officers.

A well-prepared application should be accurate, complete, and supported by genuine evidence. If a problem exists, it is generally better to understand and address it honestly than to conceal it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does spousal sponsorship take in Canada?

Processing times vary according to the application category, location, completeness of the application, and other factors. Couples should check IRCC's official processing-time tool for the latest estimate applicable to their case.

### Do I need a consultant to sponsor my spouse in Canada?

No. Couples can apply on their own using the official government instructions. A consultant is optional and may be useful when applicants want help understanding eligibility, organizing documents, or dealing with complicated circumstances.

### Can a permanent resident sponsor a spouse while living outside Canada?

Generally, a permanent resident must live in Canada to sponsor a spouse or partner. Canadian citizens living abroad may be able to sponsor a spouse if they demonstrate an intention to live in Canada when the sponsored person becomes a permanent resident.

### Is there a minimum income requirement for spousal sponsorship?

In most federal spousal sponsorship cases, there is no minimum income requirement. However, sponsors must meet their undertaking obligations, and exceptions or additional financial requirements can apply in certain circumstances.

### Can common-law partners apply for permanent residence?

Yes, if they meet the applicable requirements. Common-law partners generally must have lived together in a marriage-like relationship for at least 12 consecutive months and provide evidence supporting their relationship.

### Can my spouse work while the sponsorship application is processing?

Some spouses or common-law partners living in Canada may qualify for an open work permit under the applicable rules. Eligibility is not automatic and should be checked against current government instructions.

### Are the rules different for couples living in Quebec?

Quebec has additional provincial sponsorship requirements. Couples living in Quebec should follow the relevant federal instructions and Quebec's current process, including any required provincial undertaking.

### Can a consultant guarantee permanent residence approval?

No. Only Canadian immigration authorities can decide the application. A consultant may assist with preparation and representation where authorized, but cannot guarantee approval.
